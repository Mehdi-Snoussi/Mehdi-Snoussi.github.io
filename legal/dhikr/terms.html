3 The texts and where they come from

Dhikr’s design, code and wording are ours. The sacred texts and the works built on them belong to their authors and publishers, and Dhikr names each one under Settings, in About:

  • the Quran in Uthmani script, from the Tanzil project;
  • the English meaning by Saheeh International and the French one by the Montada Islamic Foundation;
  • the azkar, from Hisn al-Muslim, each with the collection it is reported in;
  • Al-Mukhtasar’s tafsir, served by QuranEnc, and the Forty Hadith, from HadeethEnc;

We copy sacred text letter for letter and check it against its source. If you find a mistake in an ayah, a hadith, a reference or a translation, write to us and we will correct it.

The prophets’ stories are our own retelling, read aloud by computer voices, with each ayah recited by a human reciter. The app marks them “Under review” until a scholar has read them.

4 Prayer times, the Qibla and the Hijri date

Dhikr calculates prayer times from your position, the date and the method you choose, or the method usual in your country. Mosques and authorities can differ from a calculation by a few minutes, and they are the ones to follow. You can shift each prayer by minutes in Settings.

The Qibla needle depends on your phone’s compass, which metal and magnets nearby can pull off course. The Hijri date follows the Umm al-Qura calendar; where the month begins by moon sighting it can be a day apart, and Settings lets you move it a day either way.

Dhikr is a help to worship, not an authority on it. For a ruling, ask a scholar you trust.

5 No account, and your watch

Dhikr has no sign-up. Your counts, marks, bookmarks and settings live on your phone. If you uninstall the app, clear its data or change phones without a backup or a copy file, they are gone and we cannot recover them. Remove Ads comes back through “Restore Purchases”.

Dhikr for Wear OS is part of the same app. The watch takes its prayer times from Dhikr on your phone and sends your finished rounds back to it.
